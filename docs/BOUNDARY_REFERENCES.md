# Boundary impact references

Reviewed for the boundary implementation in November 2026. Sources guide the geometry and contact model; none establishes a measured cricket-ball/foam restitution or friction coefficient for this installation.

1. [Promofoam, cricket boundary advertising wedge](https://promofoam.co.uk/icc-ecb-type-approved-cricket-boundary-advertising-wedge-2m/). The manufacturer specifies a vinyl exterior, polyurethane foam core, and a cross-section 230 mm wide and 147 mm tall. These dimensions supply the defaults in `Config/field.json`. Product approval claims are not needed by the simulation.
2. [Foams4Sports, foam advertising wedge](https://www.foams4sports.co.uk/other/advertising/foam-advertising-wedge). Describes energy-absorbing PU padding, a PVC exterior and turf securing points. This motivates modelling a stationary, dissipative cushion; its material description does not supply a numerical restitution coefficient.
3. [Mabbs and Smith (2025), *The effects of spin and material on the coefficient of restitution for oblique impacts of solid balls*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17543371251327464). Defines restitution using the normal velocity component and examines friction/spin in oblique impacts. Table 1 reports 0.56 for the tested cricket ball on a rigid steel surface at 20 m/s normal incidence. That value is **not** a foam-wedge measurement and is not used as the foam default. The study also shows why one constant does not describe every angle/material/contact condition.
4. [Cross (2021), *Oblique impact of a hard ball on a soft surface*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ac221e). Describes how deformation of a soft target affects oblique rebound. The implementation uses an effective restitution/friction model rather than claiming to reproduce the paper's deforming-surface experiment.
5. [Biber, Champneys and Szalai (2023), *Analysis of point-contact models of the bounce of a hard spinning ball on a compliant frictional surface*](https://academic.oup.com/imamat/article/88/3/498/7235664), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11685). Discusses the scope and limits of rigid-sphere, restitution and Coulomb-friction models for spinning bounce. In particular, more complex tangential compliance and slip reversal require richer contact dynamics than a single impulse.

## Implementation choices

The wedge default is restitution **0.20**, friction **0.40**. These are explicit, uncalibrated tuning choices for a strongly damped foam surface. Users can change them independently of the geometric slope. The barricade inherits the pitch's current **0.43** restitution and **0.22** friction unless overridden, as requested. This inheritance is a configurable approximation, not a claim about a specific wall material. Grass outfield coefficients are separate and do not replace the wall or terrace response.

The normal/tangential impulse calculation accounts for the ball's rotational inertia. A slope's actual normal redirects velocity; changing only horizontal speed would fail to create the upward deflection of a wedge. Friction can change both translation and spin while reducing total kinetic energy. Swept finite-radius contacts prevent a fast ball from jumping through the narrow cushion. Low-speed support handles sliding and rolling under gravity. Neither obstacle adds energy or applies an artificial inward velocity to a ball that clears its top.

To calibrate against a particular real cushion, measure incident and outgoing normal velocities, tangential velocities and spin over several speeds/angles. The current constants should not be presented as experimentally validated until those measurements are available.
